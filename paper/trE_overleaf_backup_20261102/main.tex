\begin{abstract}
The carrier--vehicle traveling salesman problem (CVTSP) couples a combinatorial visiting order with a
continuous second-order cone program (SOCP) that places the take-off and landing points of a drone
launched from a moving carrier. Learning-based solvers trained with exact SOCP rewards reach strong
solution quality, but require hundreds of millions of exact solves during training.
We propose a self-improving deep reinforcement learning framework in which the policy is trained from
sparse exact feedback: only a few routes per instance are solved exactly, the remaining routes are
scored by certified upper-bound tables derived from the exact solution, and improved routes found by
local search are distilled back into the policy by imitation learning.
On the 529 benchmark instances from the literature, the proposed method matches a POMO policy trained
with exact Gurobi rewards (mean makespan within 0.12\%) while using 15 times fewer training instances,
78 times fewer exact SOCP solves and about 30 times less solver CPU time, at identical inference cost.

\end{abstract}

\section{Problem description}\label{sec:problem}
A carrier with speed $V_c$ leaves the depot $p_0$, and a drone with speed $V_v$ and endurance $a$
visits targets $p_1,\dots,p_n$ one sortie at a time. For a fixed visiting order $\pi$, the take-off
points $s_k$, landing points $l_k$ and times solve the SOCP
\begin{align}
\min\ & \sum_{k=1}^{n} \tau_k + \sum_{k=1}^{n+1} T_k \label{eq:obj}\\
\text{s.t.}\ & \tau_k = t^{\mathrm{out}}_k + t^{\mathrm{in}}_k \le a, && k=1,\dots,n,\\
& \|s_k - p_{\pi(k)}\| \le V_v\, t^{\mathrm{out}}_k,\quad \|l_k - p_{\pi(k)}\| \le V_v\, t^{\mathrm{in}}_k, && k=1,\dots,n,\\
& \|l_k - s_k\| \le V_c\, \tau_k, && k=1,\dots,n,\\
& \|s_k - l_{k-1}\| \le V_c\, T_k \ (l_0 = p_0), \qquad \|p_0 - l_n\| \le V_c\, T_{n+1}. &&
\end{align}
The CVTSP minimises the makespan $f(\pi)$ of \eqref{eq:obj} over all orders $\pi$. \todo{notation table, remarks on symmetry: $f(\pi)=f(\pi^{\mathrm{rev}})$}

\section{Computational experiments}\label{sec:exp}
\subsection{Benchmark instances and protocol}
We use the 529 instances of Gam72 \citep{gambella2018exact}, Erd132 \citep{erdogan2021exact} and Poi325
\citep{poikonen2020mothership}, and a synthetic test set of 500 instances with $J\in\{20,40,60,80,100\}$.
At test time every model decodes greedy POMO with $\min(J,100)$ starts on eight geometric views, every
distinct order is solved exactly with Gurobi, and the best makespan is reported. Gaps follow
\citet{li2025exact}: $\Delta = 100\,(\mathrm{UB}-\mathrm{BKS})/\mathrm{UB}$, where the BKS of an instance is the best
upper bound reported by any method in the per-instance results of \citet{li2025exact} (exact methods with a 3,600--7,200\,s
limit, ILS and ten TSIH runs). Optimality is proven for 131 of the 132 instances with $n\le20$ in Gam72/Erd132 and for
56 Poi325 instances. All runs use an Intel i9-13900KF and an RTX 4090.
