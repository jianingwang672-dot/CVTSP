### Genuine Strengths
Proposition 1 and its proof are correct under the stated model, and the remark on splicing tables is right. The paper reports its worst results in the abstract, discloses that Real529 is not held out and that test500 was seen once, and refuses to treat J = 200 as a general win. The figures I checked in tab:test500, tab:gen, tab:real, tab:meta and tab:budget match the ledger and source results, including the unaugmented generalisation means (36.801, 36.336, +1.63%), which I recomputed.

### Strongest Counter-Argument
The paper presents a bound-driven training method that trades a small quality loss for a 78-fold saving. A sceptic would read the same evidence differently.

First, the bound is not what teaches the policy. In the authors' own partial ablation, bound-based REINFORCE with imitation removed stopped improving within about ten epochs, at a best validation value of 21.80 against 18.80 for the full method at epoch 43. Learning comes from imitating exactly verified experts, and late in training most of those experts do not pass the first-round certificate. At epoch 133, only 659 of 10,000 instances clear the bound test, while 41% of near-miss routes, whose bounds sit about 15% too high, turn out to be better. The method is imitation learning with a table-guided search heuristic.

Second, the deficit looks like a ceiling set by the design and not a budget trade-off. Validation stops at 17.70 and never approaches G200's 17.31. Continued training does not help. The authors' own diagnostics name the biased hybrid reward as the main cause of the gap and find G200 no more diverse.

Third, the saving is overstated. Selecting E133 took 5.18 million further solves, so the as-run ratio is about 25, not 78.

Fourth, the use case undermines itself. The network already conditions on speed and endurance, so retraining is rarely needed, and at equal test time G200 wins 407 of 500 primary instances. The one case that does need retraining, a shifted distribution, was never tested. If Proposition 1 is the real asset, the natural deployment is table search at test time, not a weaker policy.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **Use the table at test time, without or alongside learning.** The paper's stated leverage is several orders of magnitude per evaluation, yet the policy spends up to 800 SOCPs per instance and the metaheuristics score every move exactly. The refresh loop already improves a frozen policy by 1.14% in four rounds on 100 instances (`docs/random_subset_reward_experiment_20261015.md`, appendix). G200 or E133 plus table search might beat both the policy and the metaheuristics at similar time. An untrained alternation of SOCP, table and LKH is also an obvious rival. If either works, the proposition's value is independent of DRL.
2. **Reward bias as the cause of the ceiling.** A hybrid that mixes one exact cost with bounds inflated by a third or more gives the solved route a large artificial advantage. This may cause both the concentration and the plateau (M5, M6). A long from-scratch run with an unbiased reward is the discriminating test; only 20 epochs exist.
3. **Imitation with table-guided search and no dense reward.** This is the reverse of the no-imitation arm. If it matches E133, REINFORCE on the table is removable and the method is simpler (M1).
4. **Fine-tuning for the operator scenario.** For an operator who already holds a general policy, a few cheap epochs on a new distribution may be better than any from-scratch recipe. This is a reviewer's alternative for the managerial claim, separate from the authors' choice not to warm-start their method from G200.
5. **Sparse exact rewards at a higher solve rate.** Random-subset rewards at about 8 solves per instance were tested for 20 epochs only. A rival spending two or three times the calls may close more of the gap.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Mission operators who bear the extra completion time on every plan.
- Dispatchers who must trust a plan that ranks below metaheuristics on clustered targets.
- Budget holders comparing a one-time compute cost with recurring mission cost.

### Unexamined Premise
The paper assumes that training cost is the binding constraint and that solver calls measure it. A 2.6% longer mission is paid on every plan, while training is paid once per policy, and the policy covers a parameter range. Solver calls are also the largest of the reported cost ratios (78, against 15, about 30 and about 11), and the paper adopts them as primary because the time comparisons are weak. No break-even analysis compares training saved with mission time lost, and without one the claim about when the method is worthwhile cannot be evaluated.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- **Existing evidence favours the paper and is not yet in it.** `outputs/pomo_gurobi_equal_budget_20261027/equal_solves_results.md`: POMO at equal solves (3.84M) is +4.44% against E133 on validation and +5.67% on Real529 (ratio of means), and E133 is better on 354 instances, tied on 110 and worse on 65. This is the strongest answer to M11, and it should be reported whatever happens to Group A. That run ended at epoch 10 with exit code 137.
- **Selection history is clean in direction.** The refined grid moved selection from E150 to E133, which is slightly worse on the test sets (+2.52% against +2.50%; +0.12% against +0.065%). Test results were seen before the refinement, but the change did not flatter the paper.
- **The random-subset study supports the sparse-feedback case** better than the 0.32% experiment does, though it has a short horizon.
- **If pending results go the wrong way:**
  - Group A 24-hour curve: POMO was at 18.09 after 9 epochs, and at early-epoch speed could run over 30 epochs in 24 hours. If it passes 17.70 within that window on the same hardware, the wall-time claim falls and only the solver-count claim remains.
  - E133 with 8 views on test500: if it comes in near E150's +1.07%, the "main quality cost" narrative of Sec. 5.2 weakens, since +2.60% would then largely reflect unaugmented decoding. A much larger value would weaken the claim of closeness.
  - Group B: if exact search with 100 solves per instance gives much better quality, a far higher budget buys what the table cannot, and "cheap and valid, not tight" fails. Sec. 6.3 currently pre-commits to a favourable reading.
  - Group D tests expert diversity, not POMO route diversity, so it cannot confirm the diversity explanation either way.
  - Further seeds: if seed spread exceeds +0.21%, the Real529 headline is not resolved, and the J = 200 result may not repeat.
  - Merging the Vieira values is unlikely to change the ordering of methods but can remove the reported new best solutions.
